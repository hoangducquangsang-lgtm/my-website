CPSIA, REACH and Pet Toys: Ask the Right Compliance Question

By Sarah · VietPaw · Updated 2 July 2026

Short answer: CPSIA is the US law for children’s products (for children 12 and under), so a toy designed and marketed only for pets is not usually a children’s product — though marketing and labelling can change that. REACH is the EU chemicals regulation and applies to articles of every kind, pet toys included: restricted substances and Candidate List (SVHC) substances above 0.1% still matter. In the EU, pet toys also fall under the General Product Safety Regulation. The right question is which requirement applies to this product in this market.

CPSIA, REACH and GPSR in one paragraph each

CPSIA (Consumer Product Safety Improvement Act, US, 2008) sets requirements for children’s products — including limits on lead and certain phthalates, third-party testing and a Children’s Product Certificate. Whether a product is a children’s product depends on who it is designed and marketed for.

REACH (Regulation (EC) No 1907/2006) controls chemicals in the EU, including substances in imported articles. Annex XVII restrictions apply where relevant, and a Substance of Very High Concern on the Candidate List above 0.1% by weight triggers information duties to customers.

GPSR (General Product Safety Regulation (EU) 2023/988, applying since 13 December 2024) covers consumer products without their own sector rules — pet toys included. It requires products to be safe, traceable and, when placed on the EU market, linked to a responsible economic operator in the EU.

Getting to the right compliance answer, step by step

  1. Describe the finished product
    Action: Intended pet, materials, components, colours, coatings, pack and marketing text.
    Result: A reviewer can classify the product correctly.
  2. Name the markets and channels
    Action: Country, retailer or marketplace requirements.
    Result: The list of rules that actually apply.
  3. Ask for an applicability review
    Action: Which requirements apply, and which do not, for this construction.
    Result: No children’s-toy statement copied onto a pet product.
  4. Scope any testing
    Action: Substances or properties, method, sample and pass criteria.
    Result: A report that answers a defined question.
  5. Link evidence to the order
    Action: Match the report to the sample and bill of materials.
    Result: A file that survives a component change.
  6. Agree responsibilities
    Action: Who decides, who tests, who pays, what happens if a result fails.
    Result: No shipment waiting on an open question.

Connect the evidence to the order

Check the report’s product identification against the sample and bill of materials you approved. If the toy contains several components, ask which were included. A change in colour, adhesive, rope or supplier may need a fresh scope review even when the item looks the same.

Retailers may request particular test methods as a purchasing condition. Keep that commercial request separate from the legal classification, and keep the reviewed evidence with the SKU and artwork version.

Frequently asked

Do pet toys need CPSIA testing?

Usually not. CPSIA covers children’s products. A toy designed and marketed only for pets is not normally a children’s product, but marketing that appeals to children can change the assessment.

Does REACH apply to pet toys?

Yes. REACH applies to articles sold in the EU, including pet toys. Restricted substances and Candidate List substances above 0.1% by weight are the usual questions.

What is GPSR and does it cover pet toys?

The EU General Product Safety Regulation, applying since 13 December 2024. It covers consumer products without their own sector rules, including pet toys, and requires a responsible economic operator in the EU.

Is there a “REACH certificate”?

Not as a universal approval. There are test reports and declarations for defined substances and samples; check what each one covers.
