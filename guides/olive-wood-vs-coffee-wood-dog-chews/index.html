Olive Wood vs Coffee Wood Dog Chews: What Actually Differs

By Sarah · VietPaw · Updated 6 September 2026

Short answer: both are hard, untreated, non-edible wood chews, and the real differences are not about toughness. Olive wood is a commercial timber with published density and hardness figures, mainly sourced around the Mediterranean, which gives an EU buyer a short freight leg. Coffee wood is an agricultural by-product from Vietnam with no standardised timber test data, graded into size bands rather than machined, and priced and shipped from Asia. Choose on supply route, sizing range and the claims you need, not on a hardness score.

What each material is

Olive wood chew: a piece cut from the wood of the olive tree, usually from pruning or grove renewal, dried and shaped. Olive is a commercial timber species, so it appears in timber databases — roughly 980 kg/m³ dried and around 2,710 lbf on the Janka scale.

Coffee wood chew: a stick cut from the stem of a Robusta coffee plant removed during a grower's replanting cycle in Vietnam's Central Highlands, seasoned about a year, dried below 14% moisture and graded. Coffee is a crop, not a timber species, so it has no entry in those databases at all.

Both are single untreated plant materials in their standard form: no glue, coating, preservative or colouring. Both are toys, not edible chews.

Why nobody can give you a hardness comparison

This is the question we are asked most, and the honest answer is that the comparison does not exist. Olive wood has published density and Janka figures because it is a commercial timber that has been through standardised testing. Coffee is an agricultural crop; it has never been through that testing, so there is no comparable number to put beside olive's.

Any supplier quoting a Janka value for coffee wood is estimating. We would rather say so than repeat a figure nobody has measured. What we can describe is behaviour on the line and in use: seasoned coffee stem is pale, light and hard, it wears into soft fibres rather than softening, swelling or developing an odour, and density varies stem to stem — which is why sizes are graded into bands rather than machined to a number.

A powerful chewer can break a piece off either material. No supplier in this category has published test data behind the phrase “splinter-free”, and neither material supports a dental-health or digestibility claim.

Which one fits your range

Coffee wood suits you when

  • You want a plant-based hard chew with a documented material-reuse story and a verifiable batch record.
  • You need a wide sizing ladder — six standard bands XS to XXL, plus four thick-cut Gorilla sizes for power chewers.
  • You are buying container volumes, or building a private-label line where laser engraving from 50 pcs per SKU matters.
  • Your differentiator is origin: a Vietnamese agricultural by-product rather than a European timber.

Olive wood may suit you better when

  • You need small, frequent reorders into the EU — a Mediterranean-sourced olive wood has a genuine logistics advantage there.
  • Your listing copy depends on quoting published hardness or density figures.
  • You need machined, highly uniform pieces rather than graded natural variation.
  • Your customers specifically ask for olive wood by name, which is a marketing position rather than a performance one.

Compare the two quotes at the same point in the supply chain

A loose olive stick quoted ex-works in Spain and a boxed, barcoded coffee wood SKU delivered to your warehouse are not comparable prices. Match the specification, the saleable unit, the packaging, the quantity and the Incoterm, then add inspection, testing, transport, duties and handling.

For coffee wood the planning figures are: export master carton 51 × 31 × 39 cm (0.062 m³) holding 120 pieces of size M, 100 of size L or 50 of size XL packed loose; HS code commonly 4421.99; sea transit roughly 28–45 days port to port to the EU or the US, before inland legs and clearance. Hanging desiccant is cheap relative to a moisture claim and is the single most useful addition for a monsoon-season sailing.

Claims that hold for both, and claims that hold for neither

Both materials support a single-material, untreated description in their standard stick form, and both lose it the moment a rope or a printed sleeve is added — a wood-and-rope toy is a multi-component product and the rope is declared separately.

Neither supports a certified biodegradability claim for the finished toy, a carbon claim, a dental-health benefit or a digestibility claim. For coffee wood specifically we also publish no caffeine-free claim: chew sticks are cut from the stem wood rather than the bean or cherry, but no laboratory result has been produced for the material. Ask us if your market requires the test.

For supervised pet play only, not food. Select a size that cannot be swallowed whole. Remove damaged toys, loose strands or pieces, and replace worn items. Strong chewers need a larger, thicker chew such as our Gorilla line; seek veterinary advice for puppies and dogs with dental conditions.

Frequently asked

Is olive wood harder than coffee wood?

There is no published comparison. Olive wood has standardised timber figures of about 2,710 lbf Janka and 980 kg/m³ dried; coffee is an agricultural crop that has never been through that testing, so no comparable number exists. Anyone quoting one is estimating.

Which lasts longer, olive wood or coffee wood?

It depends far more on the dog than on the species. Both are hard woods that wear down gradually; a power chewer can break a piece off either. For coffee wood, match strong chewers to the thick-cut Gorilla line rather than a longer standard stick.

Is coffee wood cheaper than olive wood?

The ex-factory price is usually lower, but the landed cost depends on freight and order size. Sea transit from Vietnam runs roughly 28–45 days port to port, so for small frequent EU reorders olive wood can land cheaper despite a higher unit price.

Are both safe for dogs?

Both are untreated non-edible wood toys for supervised play. Neither supports a splinter-free, dental or digestibility claim. Choose a size the dog cannot swallow whole and replace damaged pieces.

Can I stock both in the same range?

Yes, and some buyers do — olive wood as a European-origin line and coffee wood as the wider size ladder including a power-chewer option. Keep the material declaration separate on each pack.

Do coffee wood chews need different import paperwork?

For a non-EU origin, yes: a Certificate of Origin, a phytosanitary certificate and a fumigation certificate are issued per shipment and cannot be produced afterwards. No veterinary certificate applies — this is a wood article, not an animal by-product.
