Plastic-Free Pet Toys: What Belongs in the Buying Brief?

By Sarah · VietPaw · Updated 9 August 2026

Short answer: a pet toy is plastic-free only when every component — body, core, thread, adhesive, finish, tag — contains no plastic, and a plastic-free pack is a separate claim covering the bag, sleeve, window and insert. Write both into the buying brief as a component list, choose packaging that still protects the goods in transit, and keep “plastic-free” separate from “biodegradable”, which is a different claim needing different evidence.

What plastic-free means

Plastic-free is a statement about composition: no component is made from plastic, including synthetic fibres, plastic threads, polymer adhesives, laminates and coatings. It says nothing about how the product breaks down at end of life.

A toy can look entirely natural on a shelf and still contain synthetic binding thread, an internal core or a laminated tag. If plastic-free is part of your brand promise, those small components belong in the first conversation with the factory, not in a discussion after the packaging has been printed.

Writing a plastic-free brief, step by step

  1. Decide the boundary
    Action: Toy only, retail pack, or the complete delivered unit.
    Result: One clear promise the supplier can quote.
  2. List components inside-out
    Action: Body, core, rope, thread, adhesive, finish, decoration — then bag, sleeve, label, window, insert.
    Result: A checklist the factory can confirm line by line.
  3. Choose packaging for the journey
    Action: Ask how the goods will be protected against moisture and damage in transit.
    Result: Protection that does not rely on plastic you have excluded.
  4. Approve the sample and the pack together
    Action: Confirm materials against the component list.
    Result: A reference for every repeat order.
  5. Lock substitutions
    Action: Require approval for any change of thread, coating or packaging.
    Result: The claim stays true on reorders.

Vacuum packing is a method, not a material description, and kraft-coloured paper may be coated or laminated. If your brief excludes these, resolve the alternative before approving the sample.

Keep biodegradability out of the material shortcut

Plastic-free describes composition. Biodegradable describes breakdown under particular conditions; compostable adds another set of questions. One claim does not establish the others, and FTC guidance expects environmental claims to be qualified and supported.

Where evidence is limited, precise language still works: “coffee wood stick”, “coconut-fiber outer surface”, “paper sleeve”. A customer should be able to understand exactly what each statement covers.

Frequently asked

What makes a pet toy plastic-free?

Every component — body, core, thread, adhesive, finish and tag — contains no plastic. The pack is a separate claim.

Are coffee wood chews plastic-free?

The chew itself is one untreated material with no glue, coating, preservative or colouring. Whether the retail unit is plastic-free depends on the pack you choose.

Is kraft paper packaging always plastic-free?

No. Kraft-coloured paper can be coated or laminated, and a box may have a film window. Confirm the material specification.

Can I call a plastic-free toy biodegradable?

Not on that basis. Plastic-free is about composition; biodegradable needs evidence for the finished product under stated disposal conditions.

How do I keep a plastic-free claim true on reorders?

Keep the component list with the approved sample and require written approval for any change of material, thread or packaging.
