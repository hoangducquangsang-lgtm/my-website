Quality Control for Natural Pet Toy Orders

Coffee wood follows a six-stage drying/quality protocol with five QC checkpoints. Use an approved sample, agreed acceptance criteria and batch-linked records to review your order.

Request Samples & PricingGet Wholesale Catalogue

Six process stages and five QC checkpoints

The six stages refer to the drying/quality protocol; the five QC checkpoints are controls within it. They are not interchangeable counts. VietPaw does not publish the confidential operating parameters or a detailed stage-by-stage sequence.

See the manufacturer’s current coffee wood process information. For other materials, agree the checks appropriate to the construction rather than applying a wood-drying protocol to every toy.

Inspection records to agree for your order

Review areaWhat to agree for your productRecord to request

Semi-finishedDimensions, assembly and consistency before final workIntermediate measurements and correction record

These are buyer review areas, not a disclosure of the six-stage protocol or the exact placement of its five checkpoints. Adapt the checklist to wood, coir, hemp or loofah and confirm the inspection plan for the chosen product.

Material-specific checks

ProductPriority checks
Coffee wood sticksDimension bands, visible cracks, edge finish and agreed moisture measurement
Coir ballsWinding, diameter, loose fiber, core and binding components
Hemp ropes/ballsKnots, strand shedding, dimensions and attachment/pull checks
Loofah shapesDryness, seams, attachments, filling and shape consistency
All retail packsArtwork version, product identity, warnings, count and carton data

Coffee wood moisture: below 14% before packing

Coffee wood moisture is checked on every batch: below 14% before packing. Batch moisture readings are available on request.

This is a production control, not a universal pet-safety standard or a promise that mold and cracking cannot occur. Dry storage and suitable moisture-protective packing still matter.

Ask which meter/method is used, where readings are taken, how many units are checked, when measurements happen and how deviations are handled. Calibration status and a lot-linked record should be part of the discussion if moisture is a contractual acceptance criterion.

Agree quality before the purchase order

  • Approved physical sample and current specification version.
  • Measurable dimensions, material composition and acceptable natural variation.
  • Defect definitions and agreed sampling/acceptance plan; no AQL level is assumed here.
  • Any required independent lab test or third-party inspection, scoped separately.
  • Hold/rework/rejection process and written receiving/claim terms.

Do not use the phrase SGS-certified as a substitute for a report with a named product, method, date and scope. Inspection, laboratory testing and shipment documentation serve different purposes.
